### WHY — today in plain words (added 20 Nov 2026 under my new WHY rule)
- **Why we changed the rejection rule (Amendment 1):** the first rule threw away any 4-second piece with one glitchy sensor. The two behind-ear sensors glitch constantly (bad skin contact), so we were deleting good forehead data and lost 4 of 12 people entirely. A study that silently drops a third of its people can be accused of cherry-picking who counts — so we changed the *cleaning* rule (never the labels): keep a piece when the forehead sensors are clean, and mark a glitchy ear sensor as "missing" instead of deleting the whole piece. WHY it matters: all 12 people stay in the study, and the change is written down with its reason before any model scores existed, so nobody can say we bent the rules to get better numbers.
- **Why we checked whether sensors fail more when people are tired (MNAR check):** if tired people's sensors failed more, deleting/masking would quietly remove tired examples and fake the results. They don't — failures are slightly *fewer* in tired blocks — so the cleaning isn't biasing the answer. WHY it matters: this is the first question a good examiner asks about missing data.
- **Why we checked the noise spectrum (PSD):** we claimed the ear-sensor noise is "bad contact". Muscle noise or electrical hum would mean something different and need different handling. The spectrum shows low-frequency wobble = loose contact. WHY it matters: our cleaning choice rests on a proven cause, not a guess.
- **Why the baseline scoring at chance is exciting, not a failure:** the thesis question is "do published fatigue results survive honest testing?" A simple model scoring ~50% (coin flip) on sessions it never saw is direct evidence that session-to-session differences swamp the fatigue signal for this method. WHY it matters: this is the phenomenon the whole project exists to measure — but Reviewer 2 is right that we must first prove it isn't a bug, which is why the diagnostics below are running.
- **Why the label-shuffle and joke-baseline tests:** shuffled labels must score at chance (if they don't, our scoring code is broken); a model given only "where in the session is this window" must not match the real model (if it does, we're detecting time, not fatigue). WHY: these are cheap tests that protect every later claim.
- **Why the CNN validation changed:** early stopping needs a held-out slice from the same person, but most people only have ~2 usable days, so holding out a whole day leaves nothing to train on. We validate on whole held-out *segments* of the training days instead, with a safety gap at the borders. WHY: the alternative (random windows) would leak neighbouring windows into both sides and fake good validation. The CNN's real test stays the unseen-person one (LOSO).
- **Why Reviewer 1 is now lead reviewer:** I trust Reviewer 1's judgement more (my call, recorded above). Reviewer 1 gets Reviewer 2's views too, so the panel converges instead of giving two separate opinions.

### Week 2 diagnostics + CNN — COMPLETE (20 Nov 2026). Full write-up: `docs/week2_diagnostics.md`
**The story the diagnostics tell (each test exists for a reason — WHY in the WHY section below):**
- **Within-session ceiling: 0.744.** Inside a single session, fresh-vs-tired IS separable. The signal exists.
- **Across sessions: nothing transfers.** Real session-out score 0.499 sits inside the block-permutation null [0.449–0.574] (p = 0.61) — no bug, no hidden effect.
- **Mask-only baseline: 0.530 ≈ the full EEG model's 0.515.** What little "transfers" across sessions is the recording-quality pattern, not brain features.
- **Positive control PASSED:** injected synthetic fatigue effect recovered at 0.834 (full) / 0.695 (half) — the pipeline is not blind; the null is real.
